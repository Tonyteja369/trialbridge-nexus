Important Healthcare Safety Principle
A healthcare application should not make unrestricted clinical claims simply because it uses AI, medical data,
or health-related terminology.
Teams should clearly identify whether their product provides:
• Administrative automation
• Operational support
• Monitoring
• Decision support
• Patient engagement
• Clinical information support

Where a feature could influence diagnosis, treatment, medication, triage, or another important clinical deci-
sion, appropriate human review and safety boundaries should be demonstrated.

Important Privacy & Consent Principle
Healthcare information is sensitive.
Teams should think carefully about:
• Who can access patient information
• Why that access is required
• Whether the patient has provided appropriate consent
• How information is shared
• How access is revoked
• How sensitive documents and reports are protected
• How audit history is maintained
• How data is retained or deleted where appropriate
One patient's information must never be exposed to another unauthorized user or organization.

Important Reliability Principle
Healthcare systems may be used in situations where missed information or incorrect system behavior has
significant consequences.
Teams should consider what happens when:
• A notification is not delivered
• A device stops reporting
• A laboratory event arrives twice
• A result or workflow is delayed
• An external healthcare system becomes unavailable
• A patient record is updated concurrently
• An AI/model generates incorrect information
• A staff member performs an unauthorized action
• A service fails midway through an important workflow
The system should be designed with these realities in mind.
Important AI Principle
Using an LLM API to generate healthcare text does not automatically make a healthcare system intelligent or
clinically useful.
Teams using AI should demonstrate a meaningful healthcare use case, appropriate source/context handling,
clear limitations, and safeguards against hallucination, prompt injection, unauthorized actions, or misleading
output.
Important clinical decisions should remain appropriately controlled, reviewable, and auditable.
